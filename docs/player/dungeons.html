Your first rift

The first time you arrive in a dungeon run, the rift hands you a written book, the Rift Guide. This section is its text, so you can read it before you go. It holds the rules every dungeon shares; what differs from one dungeon to the next is on the board in its entry room and on its card below.

What a rip is

A rip opens onto a timed pocket dungeon, played alone.

Cross the entry room's far doorway and your clock starts. Reach the far rip before it ends.

You are lent a Rift Pick for ore.

The sigil

A rip takes one sigil of your band, or the lowest higher band you hold.

Back out before the board's doorway and it is returned. Past it, it is spent, and a death never returns it.

The clock

A bar shows your time. You are warned at 5:00 and 1:00, then the last 10 seconds are counted. At zero the rift puts you out.

Ore, chests and catches are points. Every 2 points make the clock run 0.1x faster, up to x3 at 40 points, and never slower again.

Falls and deaths

A fall costs 15 seconds. It does not end the run.

A death ends it. No items are lost: it costs $600 and the run you paid for.

Trainers inside pay no money. What you mined and picked up is yours.

Leaving

Leave by the back rip, or the far rip at the end. Once you cross the board, that dungeon is locked to you for 60 minutes.

Logging out does not stop the rift. Your time away is charged when you return.

Relics

Some rooms hide a secret with a relic on a pedestal. Walk up and it is yours, once. It costs no clock time.

Vaults

Bank vault or strongroom: a lock of 4 digits, written on signs in other rooms of the dungeon and changed every run. 5 tries; a wrong code costs 20 seconds. 4 chests, 20 seconds each.

Puzzle vault: 4 chests, 20 seconds each.

Wishing well

Throw in items of worth: 2 throws a run, nothing under 0.2 of a point. It pays back a share, sometimes nothing.

Bait room

Leave a bait on the altar. Something follows the scent into a room of your next run, catchable.

Fishing pool

A still pool with a rod on a post, lent for the run. What bites depends on the dungeon and your band.

Egg seller

One egg a run, paid in clock time: 90 to 150 seconds by band, and you need 60 left. It hatches in your pack as you walk.

Fossil dig

Break the dig blocks with the rift pick until the rock gives up its fossil. It is worth 3 points.

The board

The entry room's board gives this dungeon's clock at each band and its legs. Read it before you cross.

How a rift run works

A rip is a tear in the world that opens onto a pocket dungeon: one timed run, played solo. You step through with a sigil, cross the dungeon's threshold, and have until the clock runs out to fight, climb, dig, catch and find your way to the far rip. Everything you pick up is yours to keep except what the rift lends you.

A rip draws its dungeon from your progress, not from where it stands. Any rip may open onto any dungeon. Your band (below) decides which are possible: a dungeon is never drawn below its first band, and some also ask for a badge (listed under each). Of the dungeons you may enter, the rift opens the one you drew least recently, and chance breaks a tie; backing out still counts as a draw. The thirteen rips on the old mine's trail are test rips and stay where they are, with more rips out in the regions (docs/mechanics/DUNGEON_DRAW.md). The figures on this page are working values, tuned as the dungeons are played; nothing here has been run on the live server yet.

Your band is set by your level cap when you enter and held for the whole run: it is the first band whose highest cap is at or above yours. Every dungeon has its own clock and its own prizes at each of the 6 bands (below); a late dungeon is not drawn early, an early one scales up with you.

  • Leave by the back rip before you cross the threshold and the sigil is returned. A death before the threshold does not return it.

  • After a run, that dungeon is locked to you for 60 minutes of server running time, counted from the threshold.
  • You are lent a Rift Pick (an iron pickaxe) that breaks ore and a few marked blocks, and nothing else. The rift takes it back when the run ends.

The clock

Each run has a clock that depends on the dungeon and your band (see each dungeon below). A bar shows it; you get a warning at 5:00 and again at 1:00, and a count for the last 10 seconds. When it reaches zero the rift closes and throws you out. A fall costs 15 seconds. A wrong guess at a puzzle, and every chest you open in a vault, costs seconds too; the price is shown in the room.

Points: the more you take, the faster the clock runs

Everything valuable you take from a run is weighed in points. Every 2 points make the clock run 0.1x faster, up to x3.0 at 40 points. Points are only ever added: the clock never slows back down. They are counted for each person separately.

Deaths and the timer

Any way of dying in a rift (the timer, a boss stage, a trainer, a Pokemon, a fall, lava) ends that run. There is no recovery claim inside a rift: no Pokemon or trainer there holds your things for you. A death costs exactly:

  • the blackout's money, $600, which nothing in the rift holds for you, so it is gone for good;
  • the run you paid a sigil for;
  • the price of trying again: another sigil, and the lockout.

Trainer battles inside a rift pay no money.

What you keep: Your items are all still yours. Nothing in a rift keeps them. Kept: everything you mined and picked up.

The hidden prize

Each dungeon may hide one prize Pokemon. It is not there every run: whether it is is rolled when you cross the threshold, and the chance rises with your band (by default 10%, 12%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%). Before band 4 it is a rare Pokemon; from band 4 it is a legendary Pokemon and only a Beast Ball will hold it. Each dungeon's card gives its own chance and kind at each band, and never the species.

The healer

Each dungeon has a safe room with a healer that restores your party.

Relics and collections

Some rooms hide a secret, and at the secret a relic stands on a pedestal: walk up and it is yours, once, for good. A relic has no price and does not speed the clock; it is a reward for looking. Find every relic in a dungeon (2 to 4 of them, per dungeon, listed below) to complete that dungeon's collection, shown as an advancement.

The six optional rooms

Most dungeons hide some of these side rooms. Each costs a little clock and gives something back. Which ones a dungeon has is on its own card.

Bank vault

A door with a code lock. The 4 digits are written on signs in other rooms of the same dungeon; the code changes every run. You have 5 tries, and a wrong code costs 20 seconds. The vault holds 4 chests; each one you open costs 20 seconds. What is inside is on the dungeon's card.
