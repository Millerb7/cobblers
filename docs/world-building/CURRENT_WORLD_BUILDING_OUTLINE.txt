COBBLERS CURRENT WORLD-BUILDING OUTLINE
Date: 2026-09-10
Branch: codex/exp-009-hex-prototype
Current PR: https://github.com/Millerb7/cobblers/pull/6

PURPOSE

This file records what the repository is currently doing for world building,
what the experiments have actually proved, and why the current generated result
is not suitable as the production campaign world.


1. CAMPAIGN WORLD GOAL

The intended result is a handcrafted, believable Pokémon-style region inside
Minecraft. Routes should feel like real landscapes with readable geography,
settlements, paths, landmarks, optional discoveries, curated Pokémon, and
deliberate progression. Cobbleverse remains the reference for the desired
Cobblemon experience. The world should support that experience rather than feel
like an empty generated Minecraft map with buildings dropped onto it.


2. CURRENT EXPERIMENTAL APPROACH

The present approach is a deterministic terrain-to-server pipeline:

1. A JSON file describes the macro layout and coordinates.
   - EXP-009 source: world/source/region.json
   - EXP-001 F4 source: world/source/exp-001/f4-region.json

2. A Python generator turns that description into raster masks.
   - EXP-009: tools/generate_region.py
   - EXP-001: tools/generate_exp001_f4.py
   - Outputs include height, land/water, beach, forest, terrain-category,
     route, and event masks.

3. A WorldPainter script imports those masks and exports a Minecraft save.
   - EXP-009: world/source/worldpainter/build-exp-009.js
   - EXP-001: world/source/worldpainter/build-exp-001.js
   - The EXP-001 test area is a finite 1,000 by 1,000-block world.

4. A generated Minecraft function edits the exported terrain and places
   structures at fixed coordinates.
   - modpack/datapacks/cobblers_campaign/data/cobblers/function/
     exp_001/f4/setup.mcfunction

5. A guarded installer copies the disposable export into the existing verified
   Cobblemon 1.8 server runtime as a separately named save.
   - Installer: server/scripts/install-exp001-world.ps1
   - Launcher: server/scripts/run-exp001-server.ps1
   - Save name: exp001-f4-pallet-prototype

The server executable and mods remain in the EXP-000 runtime because that is the
known working 100-jar Cobblemon 1.8 environment. EXP-001 has its own world save;
it does not use or overwrite the EXP-009 save.


3. CONTENT CURRENTLY GENERATED FOR F4

The F4 prototype currently attempts to create:

- a broad southern coastline and ocean;
- a Pallet Town clearing near the coast;
- north, west, and east paths;
- northern woods;
- a northeastern rocky upland;
- three offshore islands;
- an abandoned western fish hut and dock;
- an abandoned northeastern house;
- a broken boat landmark;
- an offshore Relic of Pallet Town house and reward cache.

Pallet buildings are copied from the MIT-licensed CobblemonCityTowns donor pack
and placed deliberately under the cobblers namespace. The donor pack is not
enabled for natural world generation. The current town kit includes five Pallet
houses, Oak's Lab, a town sign, a Pokémon Center, and a Poké Mart. Donor source,
license, IDs, hashes, and campaign uses are recorded in:

world/structures/manifests/pokemon-town-donors.json


4. WHAT THE PIPELINE HAS PROVED

The experiment has proved several technical capabilities:

- deterministic source masks can be generated and validated;
- WorldPainter 2.27.1 can export a Minecraft 1.21.1-compatible Anvil save;
- the save boots under the complete Cobbleverse-style Cobblemon 1.8 overlay;
- campaign-owned copies of donor NBT structures resolve correctly;
- all 13 planned F4 locations can be placed by a datapack function;
- the Pokémon Center, Poké Mart, houses, lab, events, and reward chest survive
  a server save and restart;
- separate experiment worlds can safely reuse one verified server runtime;
- repository validation passes with zero errors and zero warnings;
- the automated test suite passes all 37 tests.

These results prove assembly, compatibility, placement, and persistence. They
do not prove that the world looks or plays like a handcrafted region.


5. CLIENT PLAYTEST RESULT

The current visual result is rejected as a production-quality world-building
method. The screenshots and in-game inspection show these problems:

- The finite WorldPainter export creates severe exposed map-edge walls. At the
  western coast, terrain ends in a huge vertical dirt and stone face beside the
  ocean. This immediately breaks the illusion of a natural region.
- The terrain reads as broad masks and terraces rather than authored geology.
  Elevation changes are abrupt, repetitive, and insufficiently eroded.
- The coastline is technically curved but lacks coves, rock shelves, dunes,
  vegetation transitions, drainage, and small-scale variation.
- Offshore islands are mostly flat grassy shapes with one isolated structure.
  The Relic Island does not yet feel like a discovery or a composed location.
- Pallet Town is a collection of separated donor houses on open grass. It lacks
  convincing streets, yards, fences, gardens, elevation control, civic space,
  shoreline integration, props, and visual hierarchy.
- Paths are thin generated lines. They do not respond convincingly to terrain
  or create the layered route composition expected from a Pokémon location.
- Event locations are mechanically present but have too little authored
  environment around them. A building or marker alone does not make an event.
- Vegetation density and placement do not create believable forest depth or
  frame views toward landmarks.
- Large empty spaces remain between points of interest. Reducing the planning
  scale from 1,250 to 1,000 blocks does not solve the lack of local composition.
- The pipeline is capable of placing planned objects, but the generated terrain
  does not approach the detail or intentionality required for the campaign.


6. CURRENT EXPERIMENT STATUS

EXP-009:

- The 1,250-block hex scale was playtested and found too large and sparse for
  the current content density.
- A 1,000-block planning scale was selected for the next experiment only. It is
  not the final region scale.
- The EXP-009 world remains a disposable technical and scale prototype.

EXP-001:

- The F4 world is a disposable technical prototype, not a campaign-production
  world.
- Server boot, structure placement, and persistence succeeded.
- The client visual-quality test failed.
- Curated Route 1 Pokémon spawning has not yet been implemented or proven.
- The current terrain should not be expanded into more hexes or treated as the
  foundation of the final region.


7. WHAT IS NOT CURRENTLY COMPLETE

- a production-quality Pallet Town;
- a production-quality Route 1;
- a natural transition beyond the finite test-map boundary;
- detailed terrain sculpting and erosion;
- authored forests, shorelines, cliffs, streams, and habitat pockets;
- integrated settlement roads, yards, props, and landscaping;
- curated encounter tables;
- trainer placement and progression;
- multiplayer-safe event and reward state;
- final scale selection;
- a production campaign save.


8. PRACTICAL CONCLUSION

The current automated generator should be retained as a reproducible technical
prototype tool, but it should not remain the primary authoring method for final
terrain. Continuing to add more generated hexes would multiply the same visual
problems.

A viable next world-building approach should preserve the working server,
datapack, donor, validation, and structure-placement infrastructure while
changing how the visible world is authored. The likely next proof should be a
much smaller, high-detail vertical slice made with deliberate WorldPainter
sculpting and in-game editing:

- manually shape one coastline, town edge, and short Route 1 segment;
- use custom terrain and vegetation layers with erosion and transition passes;
- compose paths, lots, landmarks, and sightlines by hand;
- use donor buildings as parts of an authored settlement rather than dropping
  them at generated coordinates;
- add local details and event dressing in Minecraft, potentially with WorldEdit
  as a development-only tool;
- judge the result against visual references before producing additional area;
- keep scripts for repeatable export, installation, validation, and testing,
  not for deciding every aesthetic terrain feature.

No additional region-scale generation should begin until that smaller slice
demonstrates the required visual quality and establishes a realistic authoring
workflow.
