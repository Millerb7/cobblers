# The Haven Compact

**Status:** Proposed for review. This is the second narrative deliverable. It
defines story characters and motives, not dialogue, trainer data, structures,
or quest schemas.

## Public identity

The **Haven Compact** is an agreement among communities whose worlds are
collapsing. It began as mutual aid: share food, shelter, transport, medical
care, and any stable ground that could be found. “Haven” is a promise its
members made to one another. “Compact” matters because no single surviving
government could speak for everyone.

The Compact is not a disguise for conquest. Most of its members are rescue
workers, technicians, couriers, guards, or ordinary families who joined because
someone got them out alive. Its moral failure grows from a real duty of care:
once the Compact has promised to save a community, its leadership begins to
treat harm at the destination as a cost somebody must pay.

People in this world usually call it **the Compact**. Its field symbol is a
plain open ring crossed by a short bridge. Exact colors, uniforms, banners, and
structure dressing remain an art decision.

## Origin

The Compact formed after several small communities survived partial world
failures and made contact through unstable rings. Early transfers moved people
from ground that was already falling apart into apparently empty fragments.
Those rescues made the method look controllable. Each success increased the
number of people depending on another success.

Director **Elara Venn** helped turn separate rescue crews into one organization.
She lost her own town after its evacuation window closed while leaders argued
over uncertain readings. That experience shaped her rule: a decision made with
incomplete evidence can still save lives; refusing to decide can kill just as
certainly. It also created her central blind spot. She sees delay as the first
step toward abandonment, even when the uncertainty concerns people at the
destination.

The Compact did not create Hoopa or the original instability. It learned that
Hoopa's rings could bridge failing worlds, then built a steering and containment
system around that ability. Repeated compulsion destabilized Hoopa and widened
the exchanges. By the time Pallet moves, the method that saved the Compact is
also making the wider crisis worse.

## Method

The Compact's process has four parts:

1. A survey team identifies a source community and estimates how much ground
   must move with it.
2. A destination team looks for a matching volume that appears unoccupied.
3. Field anchors define the exchange boundary and route the activation back to
   Hoopa.
4. Hoopa is forced to open and steer the rings. The two places exchange rather
   than one being added to the other world.

The early method was imprecise. Later equipment improved boundary control, but
it never solved the moral or practical problem: people, Pokémon, travelers,
buried places, and ecosystems can exist outside a survey team's view. A clean
measurement is not consent. Repeated use also causes remote effects, false
readings, and larger unstable boundaries.

Pallet is the Compact's largest apparent success before the story begins. It is
also evidence that the method failed: Pallet arrived intact, while an occupied
native settlement disappeared.

## Hoopa

Hoopa is held in a containment cradle beneath the League plateau at the head of
the Rift, anchored to the `league` site at (3297, 2603). The cradle lets field
crews trigger operations elsewhere without transporting Hoopa. It also keeps
Hoopa isolated from anyone who might release it.

The player cannot physically reach Hoopa before the final Victory Road
sequence. Earlier encounters are ring effects, projected glimpses, emotional
contact through Sabrina, and evidence left by remote activations. The last
operation draws the player into the containment level. Releasing Hoopa sets
`rift_crisis_resolved` before the League opens.

This location needs a built chamber and an exact underground Y coordinate. The
terrain data currently supplies only the League footprint and nearby Rift, so
the missing build is also listed in `ARC.md`.

## Leadership and internal positions

### Director Elara Venn — keep moving people

Elara coordinates the Compact and makes the final destination decisions. She
is patient with fear, plain about casualties, and unwilling to let uncertainty
halt an evacuation. She does not enjoy the cost and does not pretend no one is
being displaced. Her argument is that a known population hours from death
cannot be sacrificed to a population that might exist at the destination.

At the Craters she crosses the line knowingly. The destination data confirms an
occupied settlement. Elara orders the operation to continue because the source
community will otherwise be lost. At the Rift she still believes stopping her
means choosing those deaths. She remains responsible without becoming cruel
for sport.

### Oren Pell — make the method safe enough

Oren is the senior engineer responsible for the anchor network. He believes the
technology can be repaired faster than the source worlds collapse. He is the
first named member to admit that Hoopa's condition is worsening, but he keeps
working because dismantling the system would end every active rescue plan.

Oren supports the Craters operation until the occupied-target reading is
confirmed. He then gives Blaine the calibration record that proves leadership
understood the risk. He stays with the equipment at the Rift to help shut it
down safely rather than simply fleeing.

### Nia Calder — people on both sides count

Nia is a field medic first encountered at Lake Viltri. She treats native
residents and Compact families without sorting them into sides. She believes
the Compact owes care to every person its rescues and every person its method
displaces.

Nia argues for a pause at Peak Pond, organizes refuge after the crater operation
fails, and becomes the public voice of the Compact's dissenters at Giovanni's
town. She helps the player because the occupied destination turns a rescue into
a deliberate exchange of victims.

### Tomas Rill — measurements can answer the moral question

Tomas leads destination surveys. He joined after a careful transfer saved his
family, and he trusts better maps more than speeches. At Peak Pond he sincerely
tries to find empty ground. The dry channel and records of the earlier
city-scale exchange show him that a destination can carry history his equipment
does not recognize.

Tomas does not immediately defect. He spends the middle story tightening
surveys and defending conditional use. At the Craters he personally verifies
the occupied target, refuses to clear it, and passes the result to Oren and the
player.

### Captain Brann Saye — finish the evacuation

Brann commands the guards assigned to field teams and the containment cradle.
He has escorted families through failed extractions and believes hesitation is
more dangerous than force. He does not hate this world. He believes its people
are already safe enough to object because Compact crews did the impossible work
of surviving first.

Brann remains with Elara through the Rift confrontation. His role is pressure
and obstruction, not sadism. After Hoopa is released, he stands down when no
operation remains to defend.

### The Aster family — the human cost of stopping

Mila and Dev Aster travel with their young son, Jo, and two partner Pokémon.
Their home is among the communities waiting for the Craters transfer. They are
not officers and do not know how the anchors work. Nia helped evacuate them once
already, and they believe the Compact because it kept its earlier promise.

They appear at Lake Viltri, Peak Pond, the Craters, and Giovanni's town. Their
presence prevents the story from turning “stop the operation” into an easy
answer. After the Rift, they remain in this world under local protection. Their
source community is still in danger.

## Internal politics

The Compact has no neat good and evil wings. Its disagreements concern what to
do before the next collapse:

- Elara and Brann believe the current evacuation must proceed, even after the
  destination is known to be occupied.
- Oren believes the method should continue only while engineering can contain
  its effects. The occupied target and Hoopa's worsening condition end that
  confidence.
- Tomas believes evidence can identify a harmless destination. The older
  exchange evidence weakens his position; the crater reading defeats it.
- Nia believes rescue creates duties on both sides. She supports voluntary
  shelter and ordinary migration while opposing another forced exchange.
- Families are divided. Some support Elara because delay means death at home;
  others follow Nia because they refuse to survive by erasing someone else.

The dissenters help stop the final operation, but they do not stop being members
of the same displaced community. Their postgame problem is how to keep the
Compact's mutual-aid promise after abandoning its coercive method.

## The sympathy beat

The player sees the Compact's side at Lake Viltri before any open conflict.
Crews are carrying medicine, repairing boats, and keeping frightened families
together. Nia treats local injuries. The Asters describe a prior rescue that
worked. Tomas can point to people who are alive because somebody accepted an
uncertain destination.

No one asks the player to approve the method at this point. The scene only
establishes that the Compact arose from rescue and that stopping it will have a
human cost.

## The point where the method becomes indefensible

At the Craters, the survey result is no longer uncertain. Tomas confirms that
the selected destination is occupied. Oren confirms that the activation is
likely to worsen Hoopa's instability. Elara understands both results and orders
the transfer to proceed because the source community is almost out of time.

That decision is the line. Earlier operations may have mixed urgency,
ignorance, and self-deception. The crater operation knowingly displaces one
population to save another and knowingly risks wider collapse. Stopping it sets
`crater_operation_stopped`; defeating Blaine separately sets `gym7_cleared`.

## After the Rift

The Haven Compact survives as people and a promise, while its forced-exchange
program ends. Nia and Oren turn its remaining supplies, routes, and records
toward ordinary shelter and contact. Tomas begins mapping every known exchanged
place with Maren. Elara faces the people she failed to evacuate and the people
her choices displaced; her final legal or civic outcome is outside the main
arc. Brann disarms his crews rather than starting a pointless last battle.

The Compact may become a postgame source of rescue, restitution, and contact
quests. It cannot resume forced exchanges without undoing the ending.
